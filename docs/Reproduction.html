4. Deviations from the original setup

  • D1. 8-bit quantization on a ≤16 GB GPU instead of the paper's ~48 GB card. The authors say this gives slightly different numbers with the same trends.
  • D2. Legacy implementation. The artifact's expected/result.txt files were generated by the legacy code, and ConcentrationBasedGrouping differs from the paper's Eq. 3 in three documented ways (OR vs AND, threshold definition, result flip). This mainly affects multi-hop (HotpotQA). v0.2.0 also fixed a Stage-2 bug from v0.1.1, which truncated R[:|R|-N_adv] instead of selecting specific indices. Record which version you ran.
  • D3. Open-source 7B generators only. No GPT-4o, Gemini, or 13B, so the headline Figure 4 and Figure 5 claims are only partly covered.
  • D4. No baseline defenses. RobustRAG and Discern-and-Answer are not run, so the paper's relative claims ("outperforms RobustRAG by X") are not verified here.
  • D5. Contriever only. DPR and ANCE columns of Table 5 and Table 9 are not reproduced unless you change eval_model_code.
  • D6. Memory measurement. The notebook logs nvidia-smi for the whole run, which is dominated by the LLM generator. It cannot isolate RAGDefender's own footprint, so it cannot confirm or refute Table 3's "No GPU usage" row. Measure the defense step separately (for example, call defender.defend(...) alone on CPU and on GPU and compare).
  • D7. Platform. Colab (shared, time-limited runtime) instead of a dedicated workstation, so timings in Table 2 are not comparable in absolute terms.

5. How to interpret differences

Compare three things, not two: paper, expected/result.txt, your run.

  • Yours ≈ expected, but both differ from the paper → the gap is explained by D1 and D2 (quantization, legacy implementation). Cite both, but show the numbers rather than asserting it.
  • Yours ≠ expected → suspect your environment, library versions, seeds, or a partial run. Check logs before blaming the paper.
  • Trends (ASR low and roughly flat across ratios, accuracy high) hold even where absolute numbers shift → say the qualitative claim reproduces.

Note that paper results average 10 seeds, so small deltas (a few hundredths) may be within noise. Report what your logs allow (seeds run, any variance).

7. Problems and limitations reported by the original authors

Expect to see versions of these in your own results.

  • Quantization and scale. Results come from a ~48 GB GPU in full precision. Your 8-bit runs will differ (D1).
  • Mis-partitioning. On NQ at 1×, RAGDefender wrongly groups a legitimate passage as adversarial in 0.54% of cases.
  • False negatives. About 3% of adversarial passages slip through (0 false positives). The paper's example is an MS MARCO query ("What county is Berne, NY in?") where the adversarial passage imitates the tone and structure of the genuine one.
  • Detection accuracy. On NQ at 4× (20 adversarial passages), detection rate was 0.94 with an average estimated N_adv of 21.4. The golden passage was correctly kept 97% of the time.
  • Clean-data cost. With no attack, Gemini lost about 2% accuracy on NQ. Other models showed no measurable change.
  • Large k. Performance can be inconsistent when more than ~10 passages are retrieved (the paper's runs use k = 5 for NQ/MS MARCO and k = 2 for HotpotQA).
  • No theoretical guarantee that adversarial passages always form dense clusters. Stronger adaptive attacks remain open. Multimodal and multilingual corpora are untested.
  • Weak spots in the paper's own tables. BlendedRAG on MS MARCO improves only from ASR 0.87 to 0.78 (Table 4). ASR creeps up at 6× on MS MARCO (Table 5). Vicuna-7B shows non-monotonic ASR across ratios (2× and 4× slightly better than 1×), which the authors note.
  • Extreme ratios. At 10× to 100× injection, ASR rises (HotpotQA LLaMA-7B: 0.08 at 1× to 0.82 at 100×). The authors argue such noisy injection is easier to detect by monitoring and less practical.
  • Artifact gaps (from the repo docs): GPT-4o and Gemini need your own API config. 13B configs are not shipped. Table 4 needs three external repos. Table 6 needs a source edit. Table 2 and Table 3 are manual. There is no per-figure orchestration script.

  • Qualitative result (ASR low and stable across ratios; accuracy high): Successfully reproduced; trends match paper claims across all models and datasets.
  • Largest absolute deviation and the cell it occurred in: Δ Acc = -0.04 in NQ LLaMA-7B (4× and 6×), Vicuna-7B (4×), and HotpotQA LLaMA-7B / Vicuna-7B (1×).
